# LiD Test Prep visual identity

## Brand idea

**Calm civic confidence.** LiD Test Prep should make a consequential exam feel understandable, achievable, and humane. It is serious without becoming institutional, modern without feeling fashionable, and encouraging without becoming childish.

The visual system balances three qualities:

- **Clarity:** strong hierarchy, direct language, and restrained interfaces.
- **Confidence:** precise typography, deliberate spacing, and dependable interaction states.
- **Warmth:** luminous color, soft depth, and encouraging feedback.

## Personality

The product is a composed study companion: informed, patient, quietly optimistic, and respectful of the learner's time. It should never feel bureaucratic, gamified for its own sake, or visually noisy.

## Logo and lockup

The connected lowercase LiD pen stroke represents learning by doing. The mint tile is the primary app mark.

- Use the mark with the `LiD Test Prep` name on brand-level screens.
- Keep the mark square with rounded corners; do not place it inside another decorative shape.
- Maintain clear space of at least one quarter of the mark's width.
- Use the full lockup on the start screen and the standalone mark for the favicon/PWA icon.
- The preferred lockup descriptor is `Learn with clarity`.

## Color

### Core palette

| Token | Value | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ink | `#0c1110` | Page canvas and deepest contrast |
| Pine | `#141a19` | Primary panels |
| Raised pine | `#1b2321` | Cards and elevated controls |
| Soft pine | `#202927` | Quiet controls and stat surfaces |
| Paper | `#f5f7f5` | Primary text |
| Mist | `#aab7b3` | Supporting text |
| Learning mint | `#58c9b5` | Primary actions and progress |
| Bright mint | `#75e0cc` | Emphasis, active state, and display accent |
| Milestone gold | `#f2c96d` | Rare celebratory or achievement accent |

Mint means progress, learning, and available action. Gold is deliberately scarce and appears only as ambient warmth or milestone emphasis. Red, green, and amber feedback colors always appear with words or symbols.

## Typography

Use the native UI sans-serif stack so the app feels immediate and performs well without a font download.

- Display headings: bold, tightly tracked, and balanced across lines.
- Screen headings: strong but smaller than the start-page promise.
- Body copy: regular weight with generous `1.45–1.65` line height.
- Labels and metadata: compact, medium-to-bold, with uppercase reserved for short eyebrow text.
- Numeric results and statistics: bold with slightly negative tracking for a confident editorial feel.

## Shape and depth

The system uses nested soft rectangles rather than pills everywhere.

- Major panels: `18–20px` radius.
- Cards and grouped controls: `13–16px` radius.
- Buttons and inputs: `10–14px` radius.
- Pills: only for tags, short readouts, or compact proof points.

Depth comes from borders, low-opacity gradients, and broad soft shadows. Avoid bright outlines, heavy glass effects, or multiple competing shadows. A fine mint highlight at the top edge gives major panels a recognizable signature.

## Graphic language

- Use simple 1.6–1.8px line icons with rounded caps and joins.
- Use subtle circles, arcs, and a quiet grid as ambient geometry; these reference structure and progress without literal government imagery.
- Use the existing official question imagery as content, framed inside the app's card system.
- Avoid stock photography, flags as decoration, mascots, and ornamental illustrations unrelated to learning.

## Components

### Start screen

The first screen uses a brand lockup, an optimistic two-line promise, three trust points, and one elevated setup card. Exam and study choices appear as rich launch cards with a clear label and a short expectation-setting caption.

### Quiz

The question is always visually dominant. Read-only status is compact; question tools and session navigation are separate grouped controls. Answer cards use calm surfaces and explicit selected/correct/wrong labels.

### Progress and catalogue

Statistics use editorial numerals and a thin mint edge. Catalogue items are quiet cards that gain depth only on interaction. Section headings use a small mint overline to reinforce rhythm across views.

### Results

Results use a contained hero surface with an emblem, a large score, and state-specific tone. Passing can feel celebratory; not passing should feel constructive rather than punitive. Review cards return quickly to learning.

### Consent and dialogs

Privacy and legal surfaces use the same radius, border, typography, and depth as the product. They are calm and explicit, never styled like warnings or advertisements.

## Motion

Motion is short and functional: `140–180ms` for hover, press, selection, and progress. Movement should explain state or improve tactility. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and avoid looping or decorative animation.

## Accessibility guardrails

- Maintain readable contrast on all text and controls.
- Keep quiz toolbar targets at least 44px high.
- Preserve visible text labels for nonstandard icons.
- Use a two-ring keyboard focus indicator.
- Never rely on color alone for meaning.
- Verify layouts at 390px mobile and at desktop width without horizontal overflow.

## Voice in the interface

Use direct, reassuring language. Prefer `Choose your learning path`, `Answer saved`, and `Practice this question`. Avoid administrative jargon, exaggerated celebration, pressure, or language that blames the learner.

## Visual review checklist

- Does the screen feel recognizably part of LiD Test Prep before reading the logo?
- Is there one clear visual entry point and one dominant next action?
- Is mint used for learning/action rather than decoration everywhere?
- Is gold rare enough to retain meaning?
- Are spacing, radius, icon weight, and typography consistent across screens?
- Do mobile and desktop compositions both feel intentional?
- Do overlays and empty/error states feel designed rather than appended?
