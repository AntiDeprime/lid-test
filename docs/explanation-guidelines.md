# Learner explanation review criteria

Review the rendered explanation together with the German prompt, all four options, the marked answer, and any image. A reusable helper fragment is not an explanation unless the final text passes these checks in context.

## Required gates

1. **Accurate and authoritative.** The marked answer and every factual, legal, historical, or institutional claim must agree with the official BAMF catalogue and, where needed, an authoritative primary source. Do not rationalize a bad answer key or present a time-sensitive fact as permanent.
2. **Specific to the question.** State the exact fact, rule, relationship, date, place, or visual feature that resolves this prompt. Copy that could sit under a different question without changing meaning is too abstract.
3. **Explains the correct answer.** Give the reason the marked option is right, not merely its wording or a statement that it “fits.”
4. **Accounts for every distractor.** State the concrete mismatch for each wrong option. Group options only when the same precise reason excludes all of them. When an option is true in another context, explain why it does not answer this prompt.

Any failure of a required gate requires a rewrite.

## Quality checks

5. **Targets the likely confusion.** Contrast similar institutions, offices, dates, rights, symbols, or terms on the dimension tested by the question.
6. **Supports transfer.** Include the smallest underlying idea that can help with a related question, but omit unrelated civic background.
7. **Minimal and non-repetitive.** Every sentence must help justify the answer or reject an actual option. Remove generic introductions, test-taking tips, repeated definitions, and broad topic notes. As a house target, use 2–4 short sentences and roughly 35–90 English words; this is an editorial limit, not a universal research threshold.
8. **Clear and self-contained.** Use plain English, define an essential German term once, prefer explicit nouns to ambiguous pronouns, and keep the tone neutral and learner-focused.

Score each check as pass or fail. An explanation passes only when all four required gates and at least seven of the eight checks pass. As a final specificity test, ask whether the same text could be pasted under another question; if it could, revise it.

## Evidence used for the criteria

- Valerie Shute's research review recommends feedback that is specific, clear, task-focused, elaborated in manageable units, and no more complex than needed: [Focus on Formative Feedback](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654307313795).
- Butler, Godbole, and Marsh found that explanations of correct answers supported transfer to new inference questions better than correct-answer feedback alone: [Explanation Feedback Is Better Than Correct Answer Feedback for Promoting Transfer of Learning](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031026).
- Ryan and colleagues compare simple right/wrong feedback with concise option-level reasons and concept-focused feedback: [Beyond right or wrong](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40037-020-00606-z).
- Butler and Roediger found that feedback reduced later recall of multiple-choice lures as if they were true: [Feedback enhances the positive effects and reduces the negative effects of multiple-choice testing](https://doi.org/10.3758/MC.36.3.604).
- The NBME item-writing guide explains one-best-answer logic and why a distractor can be true in isolation yet still be wrong for the focused lead-in: [NBME Item-Writing Guide](https://www.nbme.org/sites/default/files/2021-02/NBME_Item%20Writing%20Guide_R_6.pdf).
- BAMF is the authority for the test catalogue and answer data: [BAMF Online-Testcenter](https://oet.bamf.de/ords/oetut/f?p=514:1:0).

The studies above support explanation, specificity, misconception correction, and manageable detail. They do not establish one universally optimal sentence or word count; the length target is maintained here solely for editorial consistency.
